# 4-Week Beginner Fat-Loss Plan

**For:** a beginner client • **Frequency:** 3 sessions/week • **Equipment:** dumbbells + bodyweight • **Goal:** build a consistent training habit and a sustainable calorie burn.

> This content is for general fitness and wellness education only. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Consult a qualified healthcare professional before starting any exercise or nutrition program, especially if you have an existing health condition, are pregnant, or take medication.

## How to use this plan

- Train 3 non-consecutive days each week (e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri).
- Warm up 5 minutes before every session; cool down 5 minutes after.
- Stop any movement that causes sharp pain and check in with a qualified professional.
- Progress by adding reps first, then a little weight, as the plan notes each week.

## Weekly structure

Each session is a full-body workout. Three sessions per week labelled A, B, C.

### Week 1 — Learn the movements (focus on form)

| Session A | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Bodyweight squat | 3 × 10 |
| Incline push-up | 3 × 8 |
| Dumbbell row | 3 × 10 each side |
| Glute bridge | 3 × 12 |
| Plank | 3 × 20 sec |
| Brisk walk (finisher) | 10 min |

Sessions B and C repeat the same movements. Keep weights light; rest 60–90 sec between sets.

### Week 2 — Add volume

- Same movements, increase to **3 × 12** on squats, rows, and glute bridges.
- Push-ups: **3 × 10**. Plank: **3 × 30 sec**.
- Finisher walk: **15 min**.

### Week 3 — Add load

- Move squats and rows to a slightly heavier dumbbell, back to **3 × 10**.
- Add **goblet squat** in place of bodyweight squat if form is solid.
- Plank: **3 × 40 sec**. Finisher: 15–20 min brisk walk or light intervals.

### Week 4 — Consolidate

- Repeat Week 3 loads, aim for **3 × 12** across the main lifts.
- Add one optional 4th easy session (walk + mobility) if energy and recovery allow.
- Reassess at the end of the week: how do the movements feel, what got easier?

## Progression notes

- "Progress" means small, steady increases — a rep or two, or a small weight bump — not jumping ahead.
- A week that feels too hard is a signal to repeat it, not to push through pain.
- Recovery, sleep, and consistency matter more than any single session.

## Disclaimer

Results vary between individuals. This plan is general fitness education, not medical care. If you have any pain, dizziness, or a health condition, stop and consult a qualified healthcare professional.
