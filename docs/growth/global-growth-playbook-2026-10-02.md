# Matterhorn Desks: global discovery playbook

Status: local strategy draft, 2 October 2026. Nothing here has been published, sent or purchased. Audience assumption: English-speaking crypto researchers and small teams worldwide. No keyword-volume, traffic, conversion or revenue baseline is available.

## Position and promise

Lead with **“An AI workspace for crypto research.”** Explain the job: choose a model, open a protocol desk, ask a bounded question, and review the returned evidence. Use “cowork for crypto” as an explanatory category phrase, not a claim of Anthropic affiliation, parity or leadership.

The distinction worth proving is a usable research workflow with protocol context, source/freshness checks and explicit approval boundaries. Do not lead with autonomous trading, guaranteed returns, “best AI,” “100% private,” or worldwide unrestricted availability. Useful evidence should persuade the reader; repeated superlatives will not.

Content brief template: reader question; direct answer; required public inputs; safe prompt; actual expected tool evidence; failure/limitation; source links; one relevant next step. Claims ledger review is mandatory. A comparison with Claude Cowork or a developer toolkit requires dated primary-source comparison criteria and actual Matterhorn evidence; it is not part of this release.

## Distribution queue — preparation only

1. **Owned discovery:** after approval, add a contextual link from the main Matterhorn site and relevant repository documentation to `/learn`. Keep desks.matterhorn.so the canonical guide origin; do not replicate full pages across several company domains without a canonical strategy.
2. **Protocol education:** prepare a short example for each relevant community: problem, public inputs, how to verify the result, one honest limitation and source documentation. Ask the community owner about posting rules; do not auto-post, imply a partnership, or use a link-only pitch.
3. **Developer credibility:** prepare a technical walkthrough of the read-only path and review boundary. Cite the actual public source commit when it is published. Local unpushed commits are not public proof.
4. **Launch demonstration:** once normal-user acceptance passes, prepare a short walkthrough showing model choice → desk → evidence-backed answer → failed-read handling. Strip account names, addresses linked to people, private identifiers, tokens and sensitive prompts.
5. **Earned coverage:** offer a useful technical example to an appropriate publication only after separate outreach approval. No purchased backlinks, fake reviews, engagement exchanges, scraped mailing lists or automated replies.

Suggested unsent pitch structure: “Here is a reproducible read-only workflow for [specific research problem]. It shows [evidence check] and when the tool cannot answer. Matterhorn is in public beta; this is not trading advice or an endorsed integration. May we share the technical walkthrough under your community rules?” Replace placeholders using a verified example before sending.

## Worldwide access and localization

Use clear global English first. Avoid locale-specific assumptions in currency, date, network and protocol eligibility. Do not say that a worldwide audience means every financial action is legally available everywhere.

Choose a second language only after demand, support ownership and a native-language technical reviewer exist. A translation must include its risks and recovery instructions, not just headings. Use separate stable URLs, self-canonicals for genuinely translated pages, reciprocal hreflang including itself and the equivalent English page, and a visible language switch. No automatic IP redirects. These are future requirements, not implemented routes. See [Google's localized-version guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions).

## Answer-engine visibility

Keep a concise answer next to source-backed detail, meaningful headings, bounded examples and visible limitations. Structured data must describe the visible page. Existing `llms.txt` is a supplementary pointer, not a ranking mechanism. No hidden bot-only text, prompt-injection bait, fake FAQ/review schema or mass near-duplicate pages. Google's [AI-features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) supports ordinary search fundamentals, not an AI-specific inclusion guarantee.

After publication, a small manual citation review can test fixed neutral questions on explicitly recorded engines, versions, dates and locales. Label it a convenience sample, record absent citations too, and never treat it as global market share. Prefer available webmaster reports; do not buy a monitoring platform or automate third-party accounts without approval.

## Decision cadence

Weekly: check route/indexing errors, review search queries and the first-response funnel, choose one friction or content gap. Monthly: review retained useful work and quality, retire unsupported claims, and decide whether another language or distribution channel deserves effort. Stop promotion when onboarding, evidence quality or privacy fails. See the measurement contract for definitions; do not report success from unshipped pages.
